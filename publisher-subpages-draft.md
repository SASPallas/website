## Competitive landscape implications for positioning

Even without a dedicated competitor memo in the repo, the local product language implies a few clear positioning rules.

### 1. Do not sound like generic media databases

If the page reads like "a better contact list," the product will feel replaceable.

The sharper position is:

- not more names
- not more noise
- better fit
- clearer reasoning
- stronger timing

### 2. Do not sound like recommendation AI for its own sake

Publishers and publicists do not need another black-box score.
They need a system that explains:

- why a critic fits
- why a critic does not fit
- what signals support the recommendation
- what to do next

### 3. Keep the human relationship layer central

The value is not automated outreach.
The value is making relationship work more intelligent and less wasteful.

### 4. Translation Scout should feel editorial, not financial

The product should not read like rights arbitrage software.
It should read like a scouting system that helps editors identify books with real cross-market potential and understand why they may travel.

### 5. Both pages should inherit the same Pallas engine story

Both products should visibly use the same logic:

1. ingest dense source material
2. bring in external signals
3. map relationships and fit
4. identify what matters next
5. help experts act with more confidence

That creates consistency with the homepage without forcing the pages to sound abstract.

### Page goal

Request a Demo

#### Secondary CTA

### Why it matters

Most outreach workflows are still driven by partial memory, shared spreadsheets, old contact habits, and broad lists that hide bad fit as often as they reveal good fit.

Critic CRM turns that process into a live system that helps teams answer three questions before a pitch goes out:

- who is most likely to care
- who is a weak fit and should not be pitched
- what evidence supports that judgment

### What the product does

#### 1. Build a live critic record

Track critics as evolving profiles, not static contacts.

The system should hold:

- outlet and role
- review history
- genres and themes they respond to
- tone and style alignment
- comparable-book signals
- recent appetite and cadence

#### 2. Score fit for a specific manuscript

For each title, the system should identify:

- strong-fit critics
- borderline critics
- poor-fit critics
- reasons behind each classification

#### 3. Surface timing and relationship signals

The product should not only answer "who fits."
It should also help answer:

- who has recently shown appetite for this kind of work
- who is oversaturated
- where past interactions suggest real potential

#### 4. Turn the analysis into an outreach roadmap

The output should feel operational:

- priority pitch list
- do-not-pitch list
- rationale by critic
- relationship notes
- next actions for the publicity team

### Sample workspace section

#### Headline

**A workspace for fit, not just contacts**

#### Suggested visual story

- left rail: manuscript and campaign stages
- center panel: ranked critics with fit labels and rationale
- right panel: critic profile, comparable titles, recent review behavior, and next-best-action prompts

### Outcome section

- reduce wasted outreach to poor-fit reviewers
- improve hit rate with critics who are actually aligned
- give publicity teams a shared language for why a critic is worth pitching
- keep relationship judgment at the center instead of burying it in spreadsheets

Translation Scout helps teams move beyond anecdotal scouting. It connects foreign critical response, reviewer overlap, comparable titles, and market signals to show which books deserve deeper editorial attention and why.

#### Primary CTA

Request a Demo

#### Secondary CTA

See Sample Workspace

### Why it matters

Translation scouting is often constrained by fragmented coverage, informal networks, and too much material to review manually.

Translation Scout makes the process more legible by helping teams answer:

- which books are attracting the right kind of response abroad
- which critics abroad overlap in taste with English-language critics we trust
- which titles show signs of real travel potential
- which titles may be admired locally but are unlikely to translate across markets

### What the product does

#### 1. Ingest foreign title and review context

Pull in:

- title metadata
- publisher and territory
- critic coverage
- review excerpts and sentiment patterns
- awards, nominations, and momentum signals

#### 2. Map critic overlap across markets

The core logic should connect:

- foreign critics and publications
- UK and US critics or editors with overlapping taste
- comparable books that have already traveled

That creates a real bridge between markets instead of a generic popularity measure.

#### 3. Surface likely travel candidates

The system should rank titles based on:

- strength of reception
- quality and type of critic support
- overlap with trusted English-language taste clusters
- comparable-title patterns
- timing and market readiness

#### 4. Turn signals into scouting action

The output should help teams decide:

- which titles deserve a closer read
- which should be escalated to an editor
- which may be poor fits despite strong local attention
- where the reasoning is strong enough to act

### Outcome section

- connect foreign reception to trusted English-language taste signals
- help scouts and editors focus on the books most worth their time
